What does Card Check do?

Card Check identifies a trading card from your camera and shows what it sells for: the live TCGplayer market price by condition (Near Mint, Lightly Played, Moderately Played), a one-month price chart, and — for Pokémon — recent PSA 10, 9 and 8 sales. It works with Pokémon, Riftbound, Disney Lorcana, Yu-Gi-Oh! and One Piece, and it tells which game a card is from on its own.

How do I scan a card?

  • Scan a Card: hold the card inside the frame. Card Check reads the set code and number in the bottom corner and the name, and stops as soon as it finds the card. The shutter button takes the photo yourself.
  • Choose from Library: identify a card from a photo you already took.
  • Type it: if a card won’t read, type its name or the bottom-corner text (for example CRI 112/086) into the search box.

For the best read, keep the bottom corner of the card in focus and avoid glare across it. Sleeves are fine.

Why does it show a short list instead of one card?

Some cards exist in several versions that look the same to a camera — pattern and stamped variants, cosmos holo promos, or an original and its reprint. Card Check shows those versions right under the match so you can tap yours, instead of guessing and showing the wrong price.

Foil, holo and cold foil prices

When a card is sold in more than one printing, a switch above the prices shows each one — for example Normal $0.28 | Foil $2.15. Tap your printing and every price, the chart and your saved collection follow it.

Market and Bulk

The Market tab shows each set’s top cards and sealed products. Bulk lists the commons, uncommons and rares in a set that are worth pulling at 10¢, 25¢ or 50¢ for Pokémon, Lorcana and Riftbound, with the lowest current listing shown under each market price. Lorcana puzzle-piece inserts are there too, laid out as the puzzle — and you can scan a piece to identify it by its picture.

Selling on TCGplayer

If you sell on TCGplayer, Sell this on TCGplayer on a scan result opens your seller page for that exact card (sign in to TCGplayer in Safari first). Collection → Export for TCGplayer makes a Seller Portal import file with your quantities and today’s market prices; upload it under Pricing → Import, review it in Staged, then move it live. Card Check never asks for or stores your TCGplayer login.

A card matched wrong or didn’t read

Tap Report a wrong match on the result. The report includes what the scanner read and, if you leave it on, the photo — it’s the fastest way to get a card fixed. Prices are estimates from public marketplace data, not an appraisal.

Does the app collect any data?

No. Your photos are analyzed on your device and never uploaded, unless you choose to attach one to a wrong-match report. The app has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, and no tracking. Your collection syncs between your devices through your own private iCloud — we can't read it. Card lookups send only the card's name and number to a pricing service — never anything that identifies you. See the privacy policy for full details.
